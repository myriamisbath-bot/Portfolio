About

My background mixes monitoring and evaluation with hands-on admin and finance work, so I'm equally comfortable designing a tracking framework and chasing down a missing receipt. I've built dashboards to track project indicators, coordinated steering committee meetings, handled the paperwork behind grant payments, and cleaned up years of accounting records so partners could trust the numbers again.

On the resourcing side, I map and prioritize funding opportunities against an organization's mandate, keep that pipeline alive in a shared tool the whole team can act on, and pull together the administrative, technical and financial pieces a donor dossier needs under real deadlines, not hypothetical ones.

I'm also the person who ends up owning the numbers: cleaning raw survey data, building baseline indicators, and moving between SPSS, Stata, Eviews and Python depending on what the analysis calls for. It's the same instinct as the admin work, really, just applied to a dataset instead of a filing system.

I've also run the quieter side of operations: leadership calendars, travel and visa logistics for international conferences, supplier and vendor coordination, member databases. Nothing glamorous, but it's the layer that lets everyone else stay focused on the actual work.

That range is what drew me to the Operations Specialist role at UTTR. It reads like a job built for someone who doesn't need a single job title to feel useful.
